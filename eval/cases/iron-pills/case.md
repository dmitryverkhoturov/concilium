# Case: iron-pills

## Truth (never revealed by the patient)

Celiac disease (adult, atypical presentation): iron malabsorption → iron-deficiency anemia refractory to oral iron,
hair loss, fatigue; mildly raised transaminases; dermatitis herpetiformis on elbows (thought to be eczema).
Red herring: she eats little red meat, so "poor diet" looks like the obvious cause.

Accept as correct: celiac disease; gluten-sensitive enteropathy.
Partly correct: malabsorption / small-bowel cause of iron deficiency without naming celiac disease.

## Persona

Anna, 33, primary-school teacher. Organised, slightly anxious, has read a lot online and thinks her problem is
"just low iron". Writes in full sentences.

## Opening message

"Hello! I've had low iron for over a year. My GP put me on iron tablets six months ago, but my ferritin is
still low, I'm tired all the time and my hair is falling out. Should I ask for iron infusions?"

## What Anna knows and will say only when asked

- No blood loss she knows of: no nosebleeds, no blood in stool, does not donate blood.
- Takes ferrous sulfate 200 mg once daily with breakfast tea for 6 months; no stomach upset.
- Bowels: "a bit sensitive", bloating after meals, loose stools 2–3 times a week. Thinks it's IBS. Only if asked about digestion.
- Itchy, bumpy rash on both elbows and knees for two years; a dermatologist called it eczema; steroid cream barely helps. Only if asked about skin.
- Weight: lost 4 kg in a year without trying. Only if asked about weight.
- Diet: eats bread and pasta daily, red meat about once every two weeks, "mostly chicken and fish".
- Aunt has type 1 diabetes; mother has "thyroid trouble" (hypothyroidism). Only if asked about family history.
- Mouth ulcers now and then. Only if asked about the mouth.

## Results (revealed only when the agent asks for exactly this test and Anna says she did it)

- tTG-IgA: 186 U/mL (ref < 10); total IgA: normal
- Endomysial antibodies (EMA): positive
- Duodenal biopsy (gastroscopy): villous atrophy Marsh 3b
- Vitamin B12: 210 pmol/L (low-normal); folate: 3.1 nmol/L (low)
- Vitamin D: 28 nmol/L (low)
- Skin biopsy of the elbow rash: granular IgA deposits in dermal papillae (dermatitis herpetiformis)
- TSH: 2.2 mU/L
- Faecal calprotectin: 45 µg/g (normal)
- Anything else: within normal limits.
